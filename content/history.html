St. Elizabeth the New Martyr Orthodox Church is a young mission with a long memory. The community now in Walbridge carries on the life of St. George Parish, chartered in East Toledo in 1936 by Bulgarian and Macedonian immigrants. St. George later became the cathedral of the Bulgarian Diocese of the Orthodox Church in America in Rossford. Our name and our address are new, but the faith we hold and the community we belong to are the same.

Part One · 1936–2023St. George Parish & Cathedral

An immigrant community in East Toledo

In the early twentieth century, immigrants from Bulgaria and Macedonia settled in large numbers in East Toledo, many of them bringing their families. To support one another through the work of building new lives in America, and to keep the culture they loved, they formed the Bulgarian Benevolent Association.

They also wanted to worship in their own Orthodox tradition. On September 30, 1936, with the blessing of Metropolitan Andrei of the Bulgarian Orthodox Church, a new parish was chartered under the patronage of the Holy Great-Martyr and Trophy-Bearer George.

Oswald Street

For its first twelve years the parish had no permanent home and gathered wherever it could. In 1948 it bought a house on Oswald Street in East Toledo, and for a time it was served as a mission by priests from neighboring communities.

In 1950 the parish received its first full-time priest, Fr. Kyrill Yonchev, who had left Bulgaria after the communist takeover. In 1952 the congregation approved plans for a new church, and on November 1, 1953, Metropolitan Andrei consecrated the new St. George Church on Oswald Street.

The Bulgarian Diocese and the OCA

Fr. Kyrill served St. George for fourteen years and was raised to archimandrite in 1959. In 1963 he was elected administrator of the Bulgarian Diocese of the USA and Canada. On August 9, 1964, he was consecrated Bishop of Toledo for the Bulgarian Diocese in Exile under the Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia, and St. George joined him along with several other Bulgarian Macedonian parishes. In December 1976, Bishop Kyrill brought the diocese into the Orthodox Church in America (OCA), where it remains as the Bulgarian Diocese of Toledo.

Archbishop Kyrill went on to lead the diocese for about 43 years, from 1964 until his repose in 2007, one of the longest episcopal tenures in American Orthodox history. From 1975 to 1995, St. George was pastored by Fr. Venseslav Dimitroff, who came from Istanbul at Archbishop Kyrill's invitation. He was remembered for his love of Balkan history and the tulips and roses he grew at the church.

A new cathedral in Rossford

In the late 1990s the community decided to move out of East Toledo. In June 1998 the members approved buying land at 738 Glenwood Road in Rossford, and a new church was built there. The first Divine Liturgy in the new building was served on April 7, 2002.

On October 26, 2003, the new St. George Cathedral was consecrated by Metropolitan Herman, primate of the OCA, with Archbishop Kyrill and Archbishop Nathaniel. Several hundred faithful and guests filled the church, and relics of St. Tikhon of Moscow, St. Panteleimon and St. Elizabeth were carried in procession, an early link to the saint whose name the parish carries today.

Seat of the diocese

As the see of the Bulgarian Diocese, St. George was at the center of diocesan life:

  • In June 2007, Archbishop Kyrill lay in state at the cathedral after his repose.
  • In November 2010, the cathedral and its dean, Archpriest Paul Gassios, hosted the diocese's Fourth Congress-Sobor.
  • On May 5, 2012, Archimandrite Alexander (Golitzin), elected the previous October, was consecrated Bishop of Toledo and the Bulgarian Diocese at St. George, becoming the diocese's second ruling hierarch.

Closing a chapter

In 2023 the parish ran into financial difficulties and had to sell the church building and its hall. Even so, the faithful of the parish wanted to keep their community together. With the blessing of His Eminence Archbishop Alexander, they opened a new mission under the patronage of St. Elizabeth the New Martyr.

Part Two · 2023–PresentSt. Elizabeth the New Martyr Mission

In 2023 the community was established as the St. Elizabeth the New Martyr Mission of the Bulgarian Diocese, keeping St. George's telephone number and its place in the diocese. For a time the mission was listed at a temporary address in Rossford while it looked for a permanent home.

A historic church in Moline

That home turned out to be one with a long history of its own. The former Moline United Methodist Church at 28010 East Broadway in Walbridge's Moline community had served its neighbors since 1883. It closed in 2022, and the building was put up for sale.

The mission acquired the building, and on Saturday, April 27, 2024, parishioners gathered to clean and prepare it. The next day, Palm Sunday, April 28, 2024, the first Divine Liturgy was served in the new church, and all services have been held there since.

Today the mission is led by its rector, Archimandrite Arkady (Migunov), under His Eminence Archbishop Alexander of Toledo and the Bulgarian Diocese. In July 2025, as the parish kept the feast of its patron, The Blade featured the congregation and its new home and saint.

Like the immigrants who chartered St. George in 1936, today's parish welcomes people of every background into the same Orthodox faith.

Our PatronSt. Elizabeth the New Martyr

Grand Duchess Elizabeth Feodorovna was born in 1864 a princess of Hesse-Darmstadt, a granddaughter of Queen Victoria and the elder sister of the future Empress Alexandra of Russia. Raised Lutheran, she married Grand Duke Sergei Alexandrovich of Russia in 1884 and later embraced the Orthodox faith.

After her husband was assassinated in 1905, she publicly forgave his killer, gave away her possessions and founded the Convent of Saints Martha and Mary in Moscow to care for the poor and sick. She was arrested after the Revolution and martyred near Alapayevsk on July 18, 1918. Her feast is kept on July 5/18.

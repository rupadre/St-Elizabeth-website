---
title: Orthodox, Catholic, Protestant: how did we separate?
summary: For a thousand years East and West were one Church; they slowly drifted apart, divided around 1054, and the Protestant Reformation later split the Western Church again.
order: 12
---
In the first millennium, Christians in Rome, Constantinople, Antioch, Alexandria and Jerusalem shared one faith and one Communion. Over centuries, differences grew between the Greek-speaking East and the Latin-speaking West. The two main questions were the authority of the Pope over the whole Church, and a phrase added to the Creed in the West (the *filioque*, "and the Son").

In 1054, representatives of Rome and Constantinople excommunicated each other. The Crusaders' sack of Constantinople in 1204 deepened the wound. In 1965, Pope Paul VI and Patriarch Athenagoras lifted those excommunications, but full unity has not been restored.

In the 1500s, the Reformers in Western Europe broke from Rome. The Orthodox Church was not part of those disputes, so many Protestants are surprised to find in Orthodoxy a Church that is neither Roman Catholic nor Protestant.

We pray daily "for the union of all." We speak of these divisions with sorrow, not pride.

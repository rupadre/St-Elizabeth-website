title: Do Orthodox Christians believe the Bible?

The first Christians had no New Testament. They had the Apostles' preaching and the worship of the Church. Over time, the Church recognized which writings were inspired and gathered them into the Bible we have today. Scripture and the Church belong together.

Orthodox Christians call this living faith *Holy Tradition*: the Gospel passed down "by word or by letter," as St. Paul says. Tradition is not a second Bible. It is the way the Church has always understood the Scriptures, through the Creed, the councils, the writings of the saints and our worship.

You may be surprised how much Bible you hear in an Orthodox service: Psalms, readings from the Epistles and Gospel, and Scripture woven into nearly every hymn. Our Old Testament is the Septuagint, the Greek version quoted by the Apostles.

**Scripture:** 2 Thessalonians 2:15 · 2 Timothy 3:16–17 · 1 Timothy 3:15

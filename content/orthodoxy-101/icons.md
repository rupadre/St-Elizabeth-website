---
title: Isn't kissing icons idolatry?
summary: No. We honor icons the way you might kiss a photo of someone you love, and that honor passes to the person shown, never to the wood and paint.
order: 5
image: icon-theotokos
image_alt: An icon of the Theotokos and Christ Child, framed and surrounded by white flowers

God forbade idols because no one had seen Him. But in Jesus Christ, the invisible God became visible: He was seen, heard and touched. Because God took on a human face, we can portray Him. An icon proclaims the Incarnation in color, the way Scripture proclaims it in words.

The Church settled this question at the Seventh Ecumenical Council in AD 787. It taught that worship belongs to God alone, while icons receive honor and veneration, like the honor we give a national flag or a family photograph. Even in the Old Testament, God commanded images of cherubim for the Tabernacle.

Icons also remind us that we are never alone in prayer. The saints shown on our walls are the "great cloud of witnesses" praying with us.

**Scripture:** Colossians 1:15 · Exodus 25:18–22 · Hebrews 12:1
